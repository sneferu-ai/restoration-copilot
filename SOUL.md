# Restoration Copilot — Soul Brief

## Who is using this and what hurts?

Marco runs a one-man vintage-vehicle restoration shop out of a two-bay garage in Petaluma. It's 9:47 PM on a Tuesday. His left monitor has the parts manifest for a '69 Camaro SS open beside three eBay tabs, a Hagerty valuation, and a text thread with a junkyard in Reno. His right hand holds a caliper; his left hand works the trackpad. He just spent forty minutes haggling a brake booster from a seller in Phoenix and lost it to a faster bidder because the dashboard didn't tell him the hunt was sealed and he could move on. He is tired, he is precise, and he has eighteen jobs in flight across six decades of American iron. If a number on this screen is wrong — a budget ceiling, a coverage percentage, an API cost — he pays for it in real dollars and real hours. Honesty over politeness. Density over breath. He never wants to wonder "did that click do something."

## What does this product feel like to use, hour 14 of 14?

It feels like the bench in a master machinist's shop: warm low light, every tool in its shadowed slot, one lamp burning hot over the work, the calipers and the ledger and the parts catalog all within arm's reach without turning your head. The product I am channeling is **Linear** — not its cool indigo, but its *aliveness*: every row is a live reading, every status pip is exact, every number is tabular and right where your eye lands, the keyboard carries you between jobs without lifting your hands, and a transition snaps in at 200ms so you never wait or wonder. The warmth comes from the brand — coral and amber on a deep plum-black, the color of incandescent shop light on oiled steel — but the *discipline* is Linear's: dense, honest, keyboard-first, alive at hour fourteen.

## What does this explicitly NOT feel like?

1. NOT a Vercel marketing dashboard with a hero stat and three evenly-spaced KPI cards across the top — Marco doesn't need to be impressed, he needs to find the part.
2. NOT a Notion-y greige document surface with soft cards floating on cream — this is a workshop, not a notebook; the readings are exact and the chrome is minimal.
3. NOT a "modern SaaS" template with a floating-pill nav over a violet gradient hero and a "Welcome back, Marco" empty state — he would close the tab.
4. NOT a glassmorphic cockpit with frosted blur, neon edges, and decorative shadows — a restoration shop is honest material; blur is for dismissal, not decoration, and two shadows on one element is a tell.
